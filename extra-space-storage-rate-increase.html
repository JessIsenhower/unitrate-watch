Extra Space raised your storage rent. What should you check next?

A large increase is frustrating, but the useful question is whether your new rate is still competitive with genuinely comparable storage nearby. Start with the operator's own policy, then check the market before deciding whether to stay, negotiate, prepay, or move.

Before you accept the new rate

1. Separate base rent from insurance and fees

Compare like with like. A headline monthly bill can include insurance or other charges that make the underlying rent increase look different.

2. Check whether the same or similar unit is advertised for less

Size alone is not enough. Climate control, access, floor, drive-up availability, and promotional pricing can materially change the comparison.

3. Ask the local manager for a rate review

If current comparable pricing is materially lower, bring the evidence and ask whether the facility can reduce or postpone the increase. Extra Space's own FAQ tells customers to contact the local facility manager to discuss monthly rates.

4. Price the hassle of moving

A $20 monthly difference may not justify moving your belongings. A persistent $100 difference may. The right answer depends on the actual savings and your expected remaining storage time.

Common questions

Can Extra Space Storage raise my monthly rent?

Extra Space's published FAQ says the original monthly rate is not guaranteed for the life of the rental and that rates can be adjusted from time to time. Your rental agreement and applicable law determine the terms that apply to you.

Can I lock my current rate?

Extra Space currently says customers may prepay up to six months in advance, which locks the monthly rate during the prepaid period.

Should I move after a rate increase?

Not automatically. Compare the new rent with truly comparable alternatives and then subtract the real cost and inconvenience of moving. UnitRate Watch is designed to make that comparison for you.

What if Extra Space is advertising a similar unit for less than I pay?

Document the listing, including date and unit attributes. A lower advertised price can be useful evidence for a rate-review conversation, but promotional and new-customer pricing may not be directly equivalent to your current unit.
