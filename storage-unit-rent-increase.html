Storage rent increase guide

Your storage unit rent went up. Don’t decide based on the percentage alone.

The useful question is whether your new rate is still competitive after you account for unit type, promotions, fees, and the cost of moving your belongings.

Why this is worth checking

Advertised storage rates have been soft even while existing renters report increases.

Yardi Matrix reported U.S. advertised self-storage rates were down year over year in May 2026. That does not mean your individual increase is wrong, but it does mean a market check can be economically useful when your rate jumps.

What to do after a storage rent increase

1. Turn the increase into annual dollars

A $60 monthly increase is $720 per year. Use the calculator on the UnitRate Watch homepage to make the cost concrete.

2. Check your current facility first

Look for a same-size or closely comparable unit. Match climate control, access, floor, drive-up status, and any promotional terms.

3. Check nearby alternatives

The cheapest headline price is not automatically comparable. Focus on the real base rate, required fees, insurance or protection plans, access characteristics, and how far you would have to move.

4. Ask for a rate review before moving

If a comparable current offer is materially lower, document it and ask whether your facility can reduce, delay, or otherwise review the increase.

5. Compare savings with moving friction

Estimate truck or labor cost, your time, lock/admin charges, and the remaining months you expect to need storage. Move only when the economics justify the hassle.
