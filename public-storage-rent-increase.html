What to do with a Public Storage increase notice

1. Confirm the effective date and notice method

Public Storage's FAQ currently says at least 30 days' notice by email or mail. Save the notice and check when it was sent. If you cannot find it, ask the facility to identify the notice date and delivery method rather than relying on memory.

2. Check your facility's current advertised rates

If a similar unit is available for substantially less, document the listing. Note whether it is an online or promotional rate and whether the access, floor, climate control, and size actually match.

3. Ask for the best existing-customer rate

Use the current comparable rate as evidence and ask specifically what rate the facility is authorized to offer you today. The goal is a concrete number, not a debate about whether the increase is fair.

4. Compare annual savings with moving friction

Moving only makes sense when the expected savings outweigh truck/labor costs, admin fees, time, and the chance that a competitor's low rate is temporary.

Common questions

Can Public Storage change my monthly rate?

Public Storage's current FAQ says rentals are month-to-month and rental rates may change. Your rental agreement and applicable law determine the terms that apply to you.

How much notice does Public Storage say it gives?

Its current FAQ says at least 30 days' advance notice by email or mail.

What if I never saw a notice?

Check the email address and mailing address on your account, including spam, then ask the facility to identify when and how notice was sent. Keep records. If you need legal guidance about notice requirements in your state, consult a qualified local source.

What if a new customer can rent the same size for much less?

Capture the listing and its terms. A lower current rate is useful market evidence, but new-customer promotions and unit attributes can make apparently identical prices non-equivalent.
