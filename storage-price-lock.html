Storage price stability · checked August 22, 2026

A cheap move-in rate and a stable rate are not the same thing.

Some storage operators now publish price locks, minimum no-increase periods, or rules designed to reduce the gap between new-customer and existing-customer pricing. Those promises can be worth real money if you expect to store for more than a few months.

UnitRate Watch view

Price stability should be part of the comparison.

A renter shopping for twelve months of storage should not automatically choose the lowest first-month price. A slightly higher rate that is guaranteed for a year can beat a teaser rate that changes after move-in.

But "price lock" is not a standardized industry term. Read what is actually locked: base rent, total monthly bill, promotion-adjusted rent, or something else.

Current published examples

This is not an exhaustive ranking. These are policies UnitRate Watch could verify directly on operator-owned websites as of August 22, 2026.

Large operator

U-Haul: 1-year base-rent price lock

U-Haul says all of its owned and managed self-storage locations offer a 12-month price lock on base rent. Customers can still pay month to month; prepaying a year is not required. U-Haul says the program applies across more than 2,100 owned and managed storage locations in the U.S. and Canada.

What is locked: base monthly rent for the first 12 months.

What to verify: optional add-ons, late fees, and whether the location is actually owned or managed by U-Haul.

Regional operator

Security Public Storage: 6-month floor + 10% cap

Security Public Storage says its standard monthly rate will not increase during the first six months. It also says future rate increases are capped at 10% and that new and existing customers pay the same standard rate for a specific unit after temporary promotions are removed.

The operator also says there are no administrative fees or deposits and that $3,000 of insurance coverage is included in the quoted rate.

Important distinction: the end of a temporary promotion can make the bill rise by more than 10% without being classified by the operator as a base-rate increase.

Source: Security Public Storage pricing policy ↗

Local example · Texas

Comal Storage: minimum 12-month guarantee

Comal Storage in Bulverde, Texas says its rate guarantee lasts at least 12 months and does not require a long-term contract. Its published policy says the guarantee applies to climate-controlled, drive-up, and parking rentals.

What to verify: the exact quoted rate, any optional services, and what notice applies after the first year.

Source: Comal Storage rate-guarantee page ↗

Local example · Texas

Pleasant Hill Storage: 12-month lock on the post-promotion rate

Pleasant Hill Storage in Leander, Texas advertises a 12-month price-lock guarantee, but its wording matters: the guarantee is based on the non-discounted rate after the promotional period ends.

That is a useful example of why a renter should separate "promotion length" from "price-lock length." Both can be true at the same time.

What should you ask before renting?

QuestionWhy it matters
How long does the advertised rate last?A one-month or three-month promotion can make a facility look cheaper than its durable price.
What exact amount is guaranteed?A base-rent lock may not cover insurance, protection plans, taxes, access fees, locks, or other charges.
Does the lock begin at move-in or after the promotion?These produce different first-year costs.
Is the policy automatic or do I have to request it?Some local operators advertise guarantees with restrictions or coupon-style conditions.
Does transferring units reset the guarantee?If you resize or change unit type, the new unit may be priced under different terms.
What happens after the lock expires?A 12-month guarantee solves year-one volatility, not necessarily long-term pricing.

Price lock vs. promotion: a simple example

Suppose Facility A advertises $80 per month for three months, then $140 afterward. Facility B charges $115 and guarantees that base rate for 12 months.

Ignoring fees, Facility A costs $1,500 for the first year. Facility B costs $1,380. The apparently more expensive unit is $120 cheaper over twelve months.

This is a hypothetical illustration, not a claim about any operator. The point is to compare expected total cost over your likely storage period.

Why this matters more in self-storage than it first appears

Industry filings show that major storage operators actively manage new-customer and existing-customer pricing over time. That means price stability is not a cosmetic perk. It changes the economics of the rental.
